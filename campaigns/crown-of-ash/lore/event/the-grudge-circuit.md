From 1492, Rosa Hollis turns the Vent Games into a year-round circuit. There are two parts:
- **The Seven Roots qualifiers:** one tournament for each root, held on that root's home ground across the year. They fill a 16-seat Open at the Vent Games, and every bout is wrestled long.
- **The Midsummer Quintet:** a two-day team event for **yards**, teams of wrestlers gathered under a coach from anywhere in the city.

Both are wrestled on the Four Salts, under the rules in rules-of-the-grudge-pit.

## Why Rosa built it
- **The rail outgrew the hollow.** The 1491 Games were the first loud Games in two years and drew the biggest rail anyone remembers.
- **The Long Open needs proving.** It needs more bouts than one afternoon gives.
- **The burn-sick tin needs feeding all year,** and every event pays a fifth of its wagers into it.
- **The commons want a reason to gather that isn't a pyre.**
- **The Keeper of the Slate becomes a citywide figure,** in the very year the commons choose elders.
- **Yards, not quarters:** "wrestle for your yard, not your street." This is Rosa's deliberate answer to the Sergeant's Fall. A yard that mixes the Ashward, the Quay and the Watch can't turn into a mob.

## The Four Salts (the Games ground from 1492)
- **The rings:** four salt rings, each inside its own ten-pace chalk. Three stand in the dye-works drying yard next to the Third Vent, which the Blue Hands lend in exchange for a share of the day. The fourth is the old ring by the vent itself, now called **the Old Ash**.
- **The Old Ash** hosts the Champion's Grudge, the semifinals and the final. Each is wrestled alone, with every other ring quiet.
- **The old Bucket Line lane** holds the stalls, the slate and the betting book.
- **Rosa's Spoons** are her deputy referees, a ring each. They are old champions (Gorm Halfhand, Teo Varrow, Wynot Northcott, and Marda Ironside when she isn't wrestling), with Annet Sallow and Nella Hollis judging. Each year's champion joins the Spoons once their reign is over. Rosa referees the Old Ash.

## The Seven Roots qualifiers
- **Size:** any size, in any bracket the host likes. **Every bout is long** (the Long Open).
- **Home ground:** the host root's ground runs **one exchange longer**. At Teo's warehouse the Ash runs 6 exchanges; at the Collarhouse the Collar runs 5.
- **Seats:** each winner and runner-up takes a seat in the Open, which makes **14 seats**.
  - **One seat per wrestler.** If a wrestler who already holds a seat places again, that seat passes to the best-placed unseated wrestler of that qualifier: the semifinal loser who scored more marks in their semifinal.
- **Seats 15 and 16:** last year's champion and the challenger they named. For 1492 that's **Chadickith** and **Marda Ironside**.
- **Money:** one ember to enter, paid to the host, which goes into the host's purse. A fifth of every wager goes to the burn-sick tin.
- **What Rosa supplies:** a Spoon to referee, the slops chest of smocks and Straps, and salt for the ring.

## The Open (the Vent Games, after Highharvestide)
- **First bout:** the Champion's Grudge, alone on the Old Ash.
- **The Open:** 16 seats, and **every bout is long**. It runs in waves across the Four Salts, drawn so that every wrestler rests at least one wave between bouts:

| Wave | Bouts | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Round of 16, bouts 1–4 | the Four Salts |
| 2 | Round of 16, bouts 5–8 | the Four Salts |
| 3 | Quarterfinals 1–2 (the winners of bouts 1–4) | two rings |
| 4 | Quarterfinals 3–4 (the winners of bouts 5–8) | two rings |
| 5 | Semifinal 1 | the Old Ash, alone |
| 6 | Semifinal 2 | the Old Ash, alone |
| — | **The interval:** the egg race and the plank-walk | — |
| 7 | Final | the Old Ash, alone |

- **Purse:** champion 20 crowns, runner-up 6, semifinal losers 2 each, quarterfinal losers 1 each, and a cup of cider for each round-of-16 loser.
- **The rest of the day stays:** the Ladle Pit at noon, the egg race and the plank-walk, and the Long Soak to the curfew bell.

## The Midsummer Quintet (two days)
### Yards
- **Sign-up:** as many yards as want to enter.
- **Members:** a yard registers 5 to 7 wrestlers from anywhere: any quarter, any root, any trade. Five wrestle in each meet. Each yard is gathered round whoever its wrestlers fight *for*: a coach, a trainer, an order, a bathhouse.
- **One yard a year:** a wrestler wrestles for one yard per year.
- **Name and colours:** a yard names a captain and registers a name and a cord colour. No guild, company or house colours (Rosa's rule).
- **Built your way:** a yard may be well-rounded, with one wrestler for every ground, or specialised: all Knot, all Push.

### Day one, Midsummer Eve: the pools (Short Open)
- **Pools:** Rosa draws the yards into pools of four to six. If more yards enter than four rings can finish before the curfew bell, the pools shrink or she adds a day.
- **Dual meets:** each pair of yards in a pool wrestles a **dual meet** of five Short Open bouts, seat 1 against seat 1 through seat 5 against seat 5. Each yard sets its seat order before the pool starts.
- **Team points:** 6 for a win by pin, yield or full fall; 3 for a win on marks. A Short Open bout always produces a winner, because a tied kettle goes to sudden ash.
- **Standings:** dual wins and losses, then team points, then the head-to-head result.
- **Through to day two:** the **top 8 yards**. Pool winners go through first; the rest of the eight go to the best records.

### Day two, Midsummer: the relay knockout (Long Open)
- **One relay bout per matchup.** It's a single Long Open in which each yard swaps wrestlers between grounds.
- **Sealed line-ups:** before the bout each yard gives Rosa, in secret, who wrestles **the Rope**, **the Collar** and **the Ash**, who is held back for **the Last Fall**, and who is in **reserve**.
- **One tally:** the marks carry across every swap. A pin, a yield, a full fall or a 12-mark lead ends the whole relay.
- **Rotation:** nobody wrestles the same ground in two rounds running, so depth matters.
- **Bracket:** quarterfinals, semifinals and the final.
- **The Second Salt** (the repechage): the quarterfinal losers in each half of the bracket wrestle each other, and each winner then meets the semifinal loser from the other half. Both winners take third place.

| Stage | Relay bouts |
|---|---|
| Quarterfinals | 4 |
| Semifinals | 2 |
| Final | 1 |
| The Second Salt | 2 + 2 |
| **Total** | **11** |

- **Prizes:** **the Wet Rope**, the knotted trophy rope of the old Bucket Line, hangs over the winning yard's door for a year, and the yard gets a keg of cider. Each Second Salt winner gets a cask.
- **Keeping the crowd a festival:**
  - yard cords only;
  - the Watch keeps the rope;
  - the captain of any yard of serving soldiers swears the Ash Rule at the slate before the yard wrestles.

## Politics
- **Permission:** under King's Law, gatherings this big need the regency's blessing, and Rosa means to petition the Lamplit Council for it.
- **The hosts:** every host gains standing from its qualifier. That means the Watch, Wynot's Ashward, the Blue Hands, Teo's warehouse, the Lowfields soldiers and the hill-folk.

## DM secrets
- **House Mostana:** the Knot qualifier is held on premises the house leases. Seipora's people will watch it, and offer a purse to put the house's name on the circuit "in friendship".
- **Soldiers:** if Lorard's army is still camped in Eleasis, the Hawk qualifier brings soldiers out in numbers, and a soldiers' yard will enter the Quintet. Hask's soldiers carried the burn for decades, which is why the Fury Rule was written. The Sergeant's Fall is the nightmare.
- **Rosa's debt:** Rosa hopes the bigger Games' pitch fees will pay down her debt to House Mostana honestly. The tin is not hers, and she won't touch it.
