# The Crown of Ash
A plain, heavy circlet of black iron, cold to the touch even beside a fire. It has seven low points like a palisade and one deep socket above the brow, where ridged iron claws still curl around the empty space the Heartcoal filled. It is ugly, deliberately so. Hask had the Velun crown melted down and wanted his own to look like a soldier's thing.

- **Public:** forged in 1453 DR "by a giant's hand" for Ivoard Hask's coronation. It is used in the Ember Law rite: the claimant kneels before the Great Pyre, the High Pyrewarden sets the crown on their head, and the Coal either kindles or doesn't.
- **The rite of day 2:** at the Great Pyre, High Pyrewarden Coldwell set it on kneeling Kitwin with grey grains packed into the empty socket. The grains glowed and guttered; then, after a killing on the dais, the crown kindled orange and the crowd roared. Choir singers carried the crowned boy into the Pyrehouse. At dawn on day 3 Coldwell proclaimed him "kindled."
- **The ruling of day 3:** two Choir brothers swore before the Watch's captains that the crown was lit with grains from an iron casket in the crypt. The Watch ruled that the Coal had not chosen Kitwin.
- **Now:** the Pyrewardens keep it in the Pyrehouse. Under the regency they keep the rites but have no claim to the crown, and no one may be crowned until the Coal is broken or found.

## DM secrets
- **Okton forged it** in 1453, reforging the band from the cold-iron reliquary in which the Velun kings had kept the coal hidden in the Pyrehouse crypt for half a century. He believed, partly rightly, that it would bind the coal's hunger to its wearer alone. **Chadickith knows** (Okton admitted it in session 1).
- The grains that lit it come from the **Last Ember casket** in the crypt (pyre-ash and dust of the Heartcoal); the Choir sang them alight. Garn said it would be "kept warm" in the crypt beside the casket. Hesby saw a crowned boy carried in at the north postern.
- The claws around the socket still carry a faint smear of Queen Ylia's burned skin from 1480. Kitwin has noticed the stain and thinks it holy.
