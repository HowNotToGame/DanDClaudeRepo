Rosa's rules are chalked on the slate by the Third Vent and read aloud before each game. Her ruling is final: "and that's the long and short of it." The wrestling has its own entry, **rules-of-the-grudge-pit**.

## Who may enter
- Anyone, from Kharun or not. Medium and Small folk only; anything larger needs Rosa's leave, and she has never given it.
- Children under twelve run the children's egg heat and wrestle in the Ladle Pit, and nothing else.
- One entry per game per person.

## Money
- **Coin:** a crown is a gold piece, an ember a silver piece, a cinder a copper.
- **Entry:** the egg race and the plank-walk are free. The grudge pit takes a stake of one ember from each wrestler, and the stakes go into the purse.
- **Your stake** is your entry ember plus anything you've bet on yourself. A foul that loses you the bout sends your whole stake to the burn-sick tin.
- **Wagers** are in cinders and embers, or crowns for the bold. They're written in Rosa's book and in Annet Sallow's copy, and paid after each round. A fifth of every wager goes to the burn-sick tin.
- **Kinds of bet:**
  - **on a bout:** Rosa chalks a price before each Grip;
  - **on a game's winner;**
  - **a futures bet** on the Open's champion, priced like a parlay: long before round one and shorter after each win. On day 12 Chadickith opened at 6 to 1, was about 4 to 1 after the quarterfinal, and about evens after the semifinal. Between rounds a bettor may cash out or add at the new price, less the tin's fifth.
- **Purses** come from the pit stakes and the stalls' pitch fees, never from the wagers. Whatever is left at the curfew bell goes in the tin.

## Fouls in every game
- No blades, spells, Rite, change of shape, rage, burning or breathing fire (the Fury Rule, 1464). Anyone who does any of these is out and loses the stake to the tin.
- No ash, salt or sand to anyone's face (Annis Hollis's rule, c. 1405).
- No help from the rail. A rail-hand who interferes is put out of the Games, and their bets go to the tin.
- Where a game has a chalk line, crossing it forfeits.

## 1. The boiled-egg race
*It began as the bath-hands' hot-stone carry and was turned to eggs in 1366.*
- **The course:** forty paces along Steamside, with an egg boiled in the vent's mist balanced on a wooden spoon in one hand. The other hand stays behind your back; bringing it out is a foul.
- **Heats** of six, with the heat winners running the final. The winner of the children's heat runs the final too.
- **Checks:** a DC 10 Dexterity (Acrobatics) check at the start and another at the midpoint.
  - Each failure drops the egg and costs 5 paces of time.
  - Failing by 5 or more breaks the egg and ends the run.
  - The fewest paces lost wins, and ties run again.
  - Speed doesn't help; steady hands win.
- **Prize:** 1 crown and a dozen eggs.

## 2. The plank-walk
*The attendants' plank to the far pool, moved over the shallow end in 1366.*
- **The plank:** a foot wide and twenty feet long over the Third Vent's crack, with a rag on a pole at the far end. The steam is thick in the middle, so it's lightly obscured with difficult footing. Walk it, don't run, and touch the pole.
- **Checks:** three DC 12 Dexterity (Acrobatics) checks along the length.
  - One failure means a wobble, and you go at half pace.
  - Two failures and you fall into the shallow end, take 1d4 fire damage from the scald, and you're out.
  - The fastest time to the pole wins.
- **The bucket:** carry a full bucket of water for +2 to each DC and a doubled purse. Any failure spills it and loses the double.
- **Prize:** 1 crown (2 with the bucket) and the scald-rag from the pole.

## 3. The grudge pit
- **The Open (to 1491):** eight wrestlers, single elimination, wrestled short.
- **The Open (from 1492):** sixteen seats, filled by the Seven Roots qualifiers plus the champion and their challenger. Every bout is wrestled long, across four rings (the Four Salts). See the-grudge-circuit.
- Grudge bouts run between rounds. The rules are in rules-of-the-grudge-pit.
- **Purse:** champion 20 crowns, runner-up 6, each semifinal loser 2, each quarterfinal loser a cup of cider.
- **The Ladle Pit** (children under twelve, at noon, since about 1395) uses the Push only: go out of the circle or put a knee down and you lose. No throws and no holds on the ground. Rosa judges with a ladle instead of the spoon. Honey cakes for everyone, and an extra one for the winner.

## The Long Soak (dusk to the curfew bell)
The end of the day, kept since the Wet Games (1423–1430). It isn't a game and has no purse.
- Everyone who competed, and anyone else for a cinder, gets into the Kettle Pool. The champion goes in first.
- Two who wrestled a grudge soak side by side, and that settles it (the Ash Rule).
- **The stokers' wager:** the stokers make an endurance contest of it anyway, and Rosa lets them.
  - Every ten minutes (a "glass"), each soaker makes a Constitution saving throw: DC 10 for the first glass, then 2 higher for each glass after.
  - Fail and you climb out, red as a boiled crab. Fail by 5 or more and you've stayed too long: you take 1d4 fire damage, and Mother Seld hauls you out.
  - Resistance to fire gives advantage. Immunity lets you soak but not win: "It's a soak, not a nap."
  - Whoever is still in at the curfew bell shares the red towel and takes the first cup.

## The old games (the back of the slate)
They're off the slate now. Rosa runs one before the Soak when the rail shouts for it loudly enough, and a champion's asking usually does it.

### The Hollis Stone (c. 1338–1480)
- **The stones:** four basalt rounds by the bathhouse step, each lifted from the ash onto the Shelf, a ledge at chest height in the bathhouse wall.
  - **The Egg:** DC 8.
  - **The Loaf:** DC 12.
  - **The Kettle:** DC 16.
  - **The Old Woman:** DC 22, the vent's old cap.
- **Rules:**
  - Lifters go up the stones in order, with one Strength (Athletics) check per stone, and stop at their first failure.
  - Fail by 5 or more and you drop it: make a DC 10 Dexterity saving throw or take 1d4 bludgeoning damage to the foot.
  - The heaviest stone shelved wins. Tied lifters walk it ten paces, with another check at the same DC; if they're still tied, they share the purse.
- **Bulk:** a lifter with Powerful Build has advantage. Rosa: "The stone doesn't care how big you are, but your back does."
- **Prize:** 1 crown for the Loaf, 2 for the Kettle, and 10 for the Old Woman, unclaimed since 1478.
- **Lifted the Old Woman:** Dunny Salter (c. 1338), Hollow Harl Brennick (1404), Wynot Northcott (1459), Hesta Crall (1478).

### The Bucket Line (1403–1453)
- **The game:** teams of four. A straw **Ash Man** stuffed with dry rushes is set alight at the end of a sixty-foot lane for each team, and the team passes buckets from the vent cistern to put it out.
- **Rules:**
  - Every round, each member makes a DC 12 Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check. If at least half of the team succeed, it lands a douse, and three douses put the Ash Man out.
  - If every member fails in a round, the Ash Man flares and needs one more douse.
  - The first team to put theirs out wins. Ties go to the team with fewer failed checks.
- **The old teams:** the Bath Hands (Steamside), the Blue Hands (the dye-works), the Pit Line (the Ashward), the River (the Quay), and in some years the Collarhouse.
- **Prize:** a keg of cider, and the Wet Rope, a knotted rope hung over the winning quarter's well for a year.

### The Bellow (1431–1489)
- **The game:** stand on Rosa's tub and shout one word over the vent's roar. A child at the far end of the street raises a rag if they heard the word clearly.
- **Rules:** a Charisma (Performance) or Charisma (Intimidation) check, with DC 12 to be heard at all. The highest total that's heard wins, and ties shout again.
- **Prize:** the brass Bellow Bell and 1 crown.
- **History:** not held since the Quiet Year. Rosa won it seven times as a girl, and Dorrik Pell won the last three.
