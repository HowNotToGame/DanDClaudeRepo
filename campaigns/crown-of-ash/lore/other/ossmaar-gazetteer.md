# Ossmaar: the official picture
What a senior official in the chancery or the Watch would have in front of them, as of the evening of the eleventh day of the interregnum (1491 DR). The sources are the **1488 Hearth-Count** (Hask's tax census), the chancery's **1490 revenue and customs rolls**, the Watch **charge-books**, and the Ash Companies' **muster rolls**. Figures are rounded. Hill steadings dodge the hearth-tax, so counts for the Cinder Hills run low.

Besides these, there are about 35 more Lowfields villages and hamlets (averaging around 330 people), about 22 hill villages and steadings, and 6 shore hamlets. The average distance between neighbouring villages is about 4 mi realm-wide: 2–3 in the Lowfields, 4–6 on the shore, 6–10 in the hills, and 8–12 between March forts.

- **Age:** 38% are under fifteen, 50% are 15–49, and 12% are fifty or older. Life expectancy at birth is about 32; someone who reaches twenty can expect about 55. The Ashward does worse.
- **Literacy:** about 8% across the realm and 20% in Kharun (clerks, priests, merchants and nobles).
- **Faith:** Kelemvor is the official observance. By law every corpse is burned by the Pyrewardens within a day. Chauntea is honoured in the Lowfields, Waukeen in the Lamp Market, Tempus among the soldiers and Tymora on the docks. Calishite households keep their own household shrines.

## Economy
**Exports** (1490 customs rolls) come to about **555,000 gp** a year:
- brimstone, ~380,000
- alum, ~95,000
- salt, ~40,000
- dyes and lamp-oil, ~25,000
- spending by invalids at the hot-spring cures, ~15,000

The crown's pits produce about a third of the brimstone with convict labour; guild and private pits produce the rest with paid hands. Calishite houses buy most of the brimstone, and **House Mostana** alone takes about two fifths.

**Imports** come to about **330,000 gp**: grain ~120,000 (the realm does not fully feed Kharun), luxuries and cloth ~110,000, timber ~60,000, iron ~40,000.

- **The Pyre-tithe** is a death-fee owed to the Pyrehouse for every burning. It ranges from a single cinder for a pauper to 10 crowns for a noble, and brings the Pyrewardens about 6,000 gp a year. It is theirs, not the crown's.

- **Prices:** a loaf costs 2 cp in normal times. It reached 4–5 cp while the gates were sealed and has fallen back to about 3 cp since noon on day 9.

## Law, order and crime
- **The laws:**
  - **King's Law**, Hask's code of 1454: curfew at the evening bell, peace-bonding on the upper terraces, and honest weights.
  - **The Ember Law**, which governs the crown.
  - **Pyre Law:** all the dead are burned by the Pyrewardens.
  - **Debt Law:** anyone who defaults on more than 5 gp can be sent to the pits until the debt is worked off at 1 sp a day.
- **Enforcement:**
  - The Watch keeps order in Kharun. Elsewhere it falls to village reeves and the Ash Companies.
  - The chancery magistrate hears serious cases.
  - Sentences are fines, flogging, the pits or exile. Until 1490 they also included the Sky Gallows.
- **Kharun charge-book, 1490:** about 1,240 charges, one for every seven residents.

| Charge | Share |
|---|---|
| Curfew and peace-bond breaches | 41% |
| Theft and receiving | 22% |
| Brawling and assault | 14% |
| Smuggling and duty evasion | 9% |
| Debt default | 6% |
| Vagrancy and begging | 4% |
| Sedition ("speaking against the crown") | 3% |
| Murder and manslaughter | ~1% (9 cases) |

- **Realm-wide, 1490:** about 2,600 charges (one for every 15 people). There were 22 recorded killings (about 55 per 100,000) and about 40 highway robberies, mostly on the Shore Road east and in the March.
- **Under sentence now:** about 1,100 people in the Yellow Pits (roughly 1 in 36 of the whole population), about 40 in the Collarhouse cells and about 20 in Fort Vell's lock-up.
- **Executions:** about 2,900 at the Sky Gallows under Hask (1458 to Flamerule 1490), averaging 7–8 a month. That includes the 40 of the Velun Plot, hanged in a single tenday in 1474. Outside the Spire, the Thornwater Tithe hanged 61. There have been none since the Quiet Year began.

- Of about 40 wounded, 19 died of the "burn fever" within a night. Seven were confined raging: five were later released into the smith Okton's care, and two are still confined.

## Government and politics
- **Form of government:**
  - Under Hask the crown was absolute, served by a chancery (clerks and the royal seal), the Watch (the city), the Ash Companies (the army, under the Lord-Marshal) and the Pyrewardens (the dead and the crowning rite).
  - There was no standing council from 1453 until day 10 of the interregnum, when the noon council in the Collarhouse adopted the **Lamplit Council** as regency (thirteen lamps; an eighteen-lamp draft with regional elders goes to the first sitting on day 13). See lamplit-council.
- **Rival claims to the throne:**
  - **The Ember Law:** the Heartcoal must kindle for the new king.
  - **Velun law:** the crown passes through Velun blood.
  - **Border custom:** the sword-heir inherits.
  - **The Pyrehouse's word:** it says the king named Kitwin on his deathbed.
- **Where power sits now:**

  - **Foreign:** Factor Seipora Mostana of House Mostana, whose galleys *Sixth Daughter* and *Patient Gull* lie at anchor.
- **This hour (evening, day 11):**

  - The first sitting meets at noon on day 13: the elder seats, Kitwin (to be produced that morning), the forge ward's funding, Captain Rook's inquiry and the Choir's silver.

## Neighbours
- **North, the Lake of Steam.** It is hot along the shore but navigable. Calishite trading houses dominate the water, and **Calimshan** lies west across it, 3–6 days' sail to its eastern ports. Free lake towns on the far shores carry Ossmaar's brimstone onward.
- **West, Varresh** (campaign realm). A coastal lordship of about 12,000 under **Lord Darson Drury**. A rival brimstone shore locked in a trade war over Calishite contracts. The border is the Brine Water, a stream six miles west of Saltreach; there has been no open war since 1477.
- **East, the Barony of Thell** (campaign realm). About 18,000 people beyond the Thorn, under **Baron Osric Thell**. Thornwater's main grain supplier, and cool toward Kharun since the Thornwater Tithe, because many Thornwater families have kin across the river.
- **South-east, Hollowmarch** (campaign realm). A hill lordship of about 9,000 under the self-styled "Iron Baron" **Corvic Strand**, with a few hundred hard riders. It raided March villages twice in the last decade. The March is thinner than it has ever been while the army sits at Kharun, and the Watch knows it.
- **South, the Shaar.** Open grassland of nomad clans and caravans. It trades horses and hides at Fort Vell and raids now and then.

## Climate, health and hazards
- **Climate:** hot, dry summers and a short wet winter (Uktar to Ches). Steam fogs lie on the shore at dawn.
- **Health:** pyre-cough in the Ashward (Watch surgeons count about one Ashward child in five coughing). The hot-spring cures draw invalids from across the lake. The burn fever followed the roc. Since day 10 a trial burn ward of three cots, under Watch protection and the Watch-Captain's seal, runs in the smith Okton's cellar in Steamside; the Collarhouse cellar keeps the two worst cases.
- **Hazards:** vent gas on the shore, bandits on the eastern Shore Road, Shaar raiders, and hill wolves. The Gallowsbird is dead.

## Gaps in the records
- Hill steadings hide from the hearth-tax, so the Cinder Hills count may be a fifth too low.
- Smuggled brimstone is uncounted; estimates put it at a fifth of the trade.
- Lorard's camp followers are uncounted.
- The Pyrehouse keeps its own books (burnings, tithes and its infirmary) and has never shared them with the chancery.
