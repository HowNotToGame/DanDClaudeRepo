# The Kharun Watch ("the Redcollars")
About three hundred watchmen in blackened half-plate and red-brass gorgets, all forged at Okton's forge. Kharun has had a city guard for centuries. Ivoard Hask reorganised it as the Watch in 1454 DR. Okton has armoured it for about eighty years, under Velun kings and Hask alike. It keeps Kharun's gates, streets, gaol and harbour chain, and until last year it also manned the Sky Gallows. Hired swords like Chadickith pick up Watch work when there's more trouble than there are watchmen.

## The oath and what came of it
On the night the king died, Watch-Captain Halda Barrowe sealed the gates and declared that the Watch would "keep the peace until the Coal chooses."
- **Noon, day 3:** after two Choir brothers swore the crown had been lit with grains, Halda's six captains ruled that the Coal did **not** choose Kitwin, and publicly refused the High Pyrewarden's claim. The gates stayed sealed.
- **Day 9:** with the Coal gone from the city, the captains voted four to one to open the gates at noon on Halda's terms: the Watch keeps the city, the gates, the Collarhouse, the Pyrehouse and the Ashward's safety; Lorard's men enter by column, three hundred of them, with no pillage. Captain Hesard Rook was relieved and confined the same morning as the Choir's leak.
- **Day 10:** under the **Lamplit Council** regency (see lamplit-council) the Watch keeps the city and the gates and holds two lamps, Halda's and Captain Marda Ironside's.

## Now (evening, day 11)
- **The Collarhouse cellar** holds the two Watchmen still lost to the burn, Joris Marsh and Gwenet Marlow, under Halda's roof.
- **The cells** hold Rook's orderly Fenia and the agitator Ashlo (inner cells, double guard under Marda), with Rook confined to quarters. Lorard's former quartermaster Unara is in the Ash Companies' irons.
- **The forge:** two Watch guards on Okton's door day and night, and wardens on the ward's lane door. A Watch notice under Halda's seal in the Ashward says the ward is under Watch protection and that frightening the sick is a crime.
- **Grain:** the Watch escorts the Keep's grain carts into the Ashward.
- **Losses:** the Gallowsbird's blood took the Watch hard. Of the seven confined raging after the roc, five were released into Okton's care.

## Key people
- **Watch-Captain Halda Barrowe:** see halda-barrowe.
- **Captain Marda Ironside:** Halda's most trusted captain and the Watch's second lamp. She commands the cell guard and the wardens at the forge ward.
- **The other captains:** Yarelle Pike, Junis Greaves, Tamley Mercer and Branen Underhill. **Hesard Rook** is relieved and confined (see hesard-rook).
- **Sergeant Wilash Redhand** (half-orc, 40s): Chadickith's sparring partner for twenty years. Chadickith says he taught Redhand everything. Redhand says it was the other way round, and that he taught the big lump to fight dirty and "small." Gruff and loyal, with a laugh like a dropped anvil. He guarded Isine at the triage tent and took it over with Halda's orderlies when she went to the forge. *Wants:* his younger brother, a soldier in the Ash Companies, alive when this is over.

## For the PC
- The Watch is a source of paid work, gate passes, gaol access and gossip.
- Five of its men who survived the roc's blood (Sergeant Hesby Bramble, Kita Dunmore, Gwenis Dunmore, Cassmund Furrow and Samson Cobb) now belong to Okton's Order, kept on the rolls as "ill" (see order-of-the-banked-fire).

## DM secrets
- **Halda's secrets** are in her own entry (halda-barrowe).
- **Redhand is Lorard's informer.** He passes Watch news to the Lord-Marshal's quartermaster's office to keep his brother safe. He likes Chadickith, but if it comes down to his brother or the boy, he will sell the boy out.
- **Marda Ironside** holds, sealed in her own strongbox, her written record of Rook's confession to Lorard (that Lorard is Pellot Rook's son). She promised Chadickith to keep it unread until day 14, then give it to Halda.
- If Okton turns against the Watch, the Watch turns against Okton's ward. Halda knows which side of that arithmetic she's on.
