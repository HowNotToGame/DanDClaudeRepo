# The Pyrewardens (the Order of the Last Ember)
Undertakers, executioners' chaplains, keepers of the dead. A realm that executed so many needed someone to make sure the dead stayed dead, so the Pyrewardens burn every corpse in Kharun to keep anything from rising. They are formally sworn to **Kelemvor**, Lord of the Dead, and most of the rank and file are sincere, overworked and quietly decent. The order numbers about sixty wardens, with twice as many acolytes and lay ash-bearers.

## Public role
- They run the Pyrehouse and the Great Pyre, tend the dying in a pauper infirmary, and keep the Long Smoke burning.
- They are custodians of the **Crown of Ash** and of the Ember Law coronation rite.
- They read the last rites at executions. Until the Quiet Year they performed the "Ash Anointing" of the condemned at the Sky Gallows.
- They back **Kitwin**, whom they raised, on the High Pyrewarden's testimony that the king named him.

## Key people
- **High Pyrewarden Tamius Coldwell:** see tamius-coldwell.
- **Sister Ilsa:** the singers' cantor. She led Kitwin off the dais on the night of the rite, and led the Ashward crowd to Okton's forge on day 9 until it turned on her.

## Since the king died
- **The Choir:** the Pyrehouse's singers in grey, an inner circle around the High Pyrewarden. One of them, Brother Peret, was caught at the almshouse dead-house with a stiletto, ash wafers and a list of Ashward names. Two others, Brothers Vesan and Wyn, swore before the Watch's captains that the Choir lit the crown with grains from an iron casket in the crypt. At the rite, Brother Garn killed an old volunteer on the dais to make the crown kindle. He later died to the Gallowsbird.
- **The rite and the proclamations (days 2 and 3):** Coldwell crowned Kitwin and proclaimed him "kindled." The Watch refused the claim at noon on day 3.
- **The Long Smoke turned green-grey (dusk, day 9)** as Coldwell proclaimed that the smith and the Watch had stolen the Coal. The Ashward crowd that marched on the forge turned instead. The street now says the Choir lied about the theft, and that it was the Choir's knife that cut Isine.
- **The regency (day 10)** leaves the Pyrehouse the rites and the burning of the dead, with no claim to the crown, and one lamp for a warden named by the High Pyrewarden (not yet named). Coldwell voted against it.
- **The forge ward (days 10 and 11):** Choir minders tore down the ward's posters and whispered that "the giant makes monsters of the sick." Someone laid a line of grey ash and a flame-stamped silver coin across the ward's door. The Watch now calls frightening the sick a crime.
- **Kitwin** is to be produced to the council on the morning of the first sitting (day 13), in the Pyrehouse's presence.

## DM secrets
- **The Glad Choir:** an inner circle of about eleven senior wardens, led by Coldwell. It was founded around 1398 DR by the Bhaalist survivor of Saradush who sold the coal to King Uzhar Velun. The order's formal name, the Last Ember, was once the Choir's name for the coal; ordinary wardens think it means the pyre. The Choir served the Velun kings for half a century. Then, judging the last of them too sparing with his executions, it opened the Pyrehouse to Hask on the Night of Cinders. They know what the Heartcoal is and worship the Lord of Murder through it; Bhaal is the god behind their Kelemvorite masks. They introduced the Ash Anointing around 1401. Once they realised whose son Kitwin was, they deliberately raised him under the smoke.
- **The Choir's faith (v2):** they believe the burn can be **mastered and shared**. Their communion of ash, their hymns and the casket of grains are a rival to Okton's Quenching. Most of the willing die; the survivors become "Singers" who hold the rage under song. Garn's calm smile was a man holding back a storm.
- **The Last Ember casket:** an iron box of grains (pyre-ash and dust of the Heartcoal) in the crypt below the sanctum. Coldwell wears its key. A bricked "plague road" tunnel behind the lye barrels in the Ashward almshouse's dead-house runs to the crypt's iron gate, and Chadickith has Peret's key to that gate. The Pyrehouse's north postern by the charnel wall is the singers' door; its guard was doubled after Hesby was seen watching it.
- **The wafers are cursed** (Grim Psychometry, day 10): steeped in grains and blood-marked, they infect whoever eats one, and tether the eater's rage to the Choir's hymn, so while the Choir sings the eater cannot refuse the song. An old, dead-god tang lingers in the residue. The Choir gives them to dying Ashward paupers "who ask to go gladly", with a narrow blade and a hymn: about forty-one since the Quiet Year, by the brothers' count. The dead smile.
- **The flame-stamped coins** each carry a thread of ash-mark tied to the casket, so the Choir can feel roughly where they are (see tamius-coldwell for where they are now).
- **Their agents:** a grey-shawled woman took reports at the chandler's by the dye-works, where the loft held the Choir's signal lamp (found empty on day 9, with a grey shawl left behind). The Choir's silver reached Rook's orderly, Lorard's quartermaster Unara and the agitator Ashlo. A Choir scout who followed the Coal out of the city was killed on the Gallows Road and left hanged as a bandit.
- **Coldwell invented the deathbed naming.** Isine's testimony has exposed it to the council's leaders but not yet to the street.
- **Okton's blood rite:** their whisper that "the giant makes monsters of the sick" suggests they know of it. They want his blood to make Banked of their own with no temper at all (see order-of-the-banked-fire).
- **The Ember Rolls:** the Choir's ledger of every anointed death since about 1401, kept in the crypt. Its early pages bear the seals of Kings Uzhar and Tazhar Velun, which makes it proof of the Velun dynasty's guilt (see oselle-velun).
- The ordinary wardens know nothing. Exposing the Choir would topple Kitwin's claim. It would also leave Kharun with no one willing to burn its dead.
