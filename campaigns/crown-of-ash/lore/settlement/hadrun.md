# Hadrun
A shepherd and charcoal-burner village high in the Cinder Hills, two days' walk inland from Kharun: terraced barley, goat-folds, charcoal mounds smoking on the slopes. Before 1423 DR it held about two hundred people. Most were human, with a handful of loxodon families whose great-grandparents came north out of the Shaar, so long ago that even they argue about why. Chadickith was born here in 1415 DR to **Berov** and **Radanya**, loxodon herders.

## The Day of the Shadow (spring 1423 DR)
The roc the hill-folk now call the Gallowsbird came down on Hadrun out of a clear sky. It tore roofs off and carried off livestock and people, killing far beyond what it could eat. Okton, riding patrol for Kharun's guard, reached the village before the slaughter was finished. He fought the beast in the burning square until it fled, one wing scorched and its beak split. About sixty villagers died, among them Berov and Radanya. Okton took their eight-year-old son to Kharun.

## Hadrun now (1491 DR)
- Around ninety people. Almost all the human survivors of 1423 are dead. Their children and grandchildren ring the bell and tell the story secondhand, and it has grown larger and stranger in the telling.
- Half the old houses are still roofless, made into stone cairns for the dead.
- **The Roc Bell**, an old bronze pot-bell on the ridge, is rung whenever the great shadow is seen. Children are taught to run for the root-cellars at its first stroke.
- At the village edge stands a crude statue of a giant, built of charcoal-blackened stones by survivors decades ago and worn by weather since. Some villagers leave offerings at it. Others spit at it, because Okton came too late for their families.
- **Dobrila**, a very old loxodon weaver and Radanya's friend, is one of the few living survivors (loxodons live long). She keeps a box of Berov and Radanya's things: Berov's carved tusk-comb, Radanya's clay cup, and a letter she cannot read. She will call Chadickith by a childhood pet-name in front of anyone.
- The bell rang four times this season, after the Sky Gallows went quiet and the Gallowsbird hunted the high cones again. The headman sent runners to Kharun begging for Okton, and the sealed gates turned them back.
- **The Gallowsbird is dead** (night of day 2, killed by Okton at Kharun's Great Pyre). By day 5 the charcoal-burners below Dunmarrow said the Roc Bell had been quiet for three days, and had heard rumour the bird was gone. Whether Hadrun believes it yet, and how it will take the news that the giant it spits at finally finished the job, is still to be played.

## DM secrets
- **The 1423 attack was not random.** That spring King Uzhar Velun crushed the Burners' Rising, a revolt of hill charcoal-burners and shepherds against his fuel levy. He sent fifty of the condemned to the Sky Gallows in a single tenday, all anointed with pyre-ash. The roc gorged on flesh anointed with the Coal's pyre-ash and went into a frenzy, and Hadrun was the nearest village to its eyrie. **The Velun dynasty's executions killed Chadickith's parents**, and Hask's reign later continued the same cycle. Oselle Velun is Uzhar's great-granddaughter.
- *Optional twist:* Berov's younger brother was one of the fifty. That is why the family was out in the fields that morning, waiting for word that never came.
- The letter in Dobrila's box is from Radanya to a cousin in Thornwater, written that spring. It is one ordinary page about the weather and a sick goat, plus a line saying the great bird had been "circling the Spire every day this month, fat and wrong."
- Hadrun's charcoal has fed the Pyrehouse fires for generations. The village sold the fuel that burned the ash that made the monster.
