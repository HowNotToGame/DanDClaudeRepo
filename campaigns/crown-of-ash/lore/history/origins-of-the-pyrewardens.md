# Origins of the Pyrewardens (c. 1240-1454 DR)

## Timeline
| Year (DR) | Event |
|---|---|
| c. 1240 | **The Grey Cough.** A plague empties Kharun's lower town. Corpses are left to lie because nobody will touch them, and some of the dead do not stay down. A cartwright's widow, **Kasette Mercer**, and eleven volunteers collect and burn the dead day and night for a season. |
| c. 1241 | When the last pyre of the plague burns out, Mercer's band kneels at the ashes and swears a vow to **Kelemvor**: *"No dead shall walk while an ember is lit, and no ember shall be lit that is not for the dead."* This is the **Oath of the Last Ember**, the order's earliest creed. The King grants them the pyre-ground on the terrace above the Ashward. |
| c. 1260-1390 | The wardens grow into a respected order: they burn Kharun's dead, run a pauper infirmary, and ward off the restless dead of the hills. Burial is gradually abandoned in the city because of the plague memory, and cremation becomes the custom. |
| c. 1398 | A newcomer from the south, **Thorim Strakeln**, takes the Oath, rises fast, and becomes the guiding hand of an inner circle. |
| c. 1401 | At King Uzhar's request, the wardens begin the **Ash Anointing**: a last rite marking the condemned with pyre-ash "so that Kelemvor may find them." Most wardens believe it is mercy. |
| 1453 | The Pyrehouse's doors are opened to Hask on the Night of Cinders. |
| 1454 | Hask makes the Pyrewardens sole keepers of the dead of Ossmaar. |

## What the order believes about itself
- Founded by the living who would not leave the dead on the street.
- The pyre is a duty, not a power. "The last ember" means the final spark of a pyre when the work is done.
- Their strictest rule: a warden never lights a fire for the living except to warm the sick.

## DM secrets
- **Strakeln** was a Bhaalist survivor of Yaga-Shura's camp at Saradush (1369) who carried the burned-out heart-coal east. He sold it to Uzhar and then took the Kelemvor oath as a mask. He founded the **Glad Choir** within the order and gave its secret name, "the Last Ember", to the coal; ordinary wardens think the name was Mercer's.
- He died in 1426, old and smiling, in the crypt, and is buried in an unmarked niche behind the iron casket. The Choir sings his name once a year.
- Mercer's own journal, a plain account of the plague, is in the Pyrehouse library. It describes the first dead who did not stay down in terms close to the early signs of the burn. Left open on purpose: an old foreshadowing of something fire-borne, or simply plague.
- Coldwell is the Choir's fifth leader.
