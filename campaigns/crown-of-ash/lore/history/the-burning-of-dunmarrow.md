# The Burning of Dunmarrow (1463 DR)

## Timeline
| Year (DR) | Event |
|---|---|
| c. 1300 | A Velun grant makes the **Dunmar** family wardens of the high eastern cones, holding the dry-stone keep above the Dunmar Road. |
| 1453 | The Night of Cinders. Lord **Denchton Dunmar** swears to Hask with the other hill lords, grudgingly. |
| 1460 | Hask lays a **tithe** on the hill lords: grain, charcoal, and a fixed number of prisoners and debtors every year, to be sent to Kharun for the Spire. The hill lords pay, except Dunmar. |
| 1462 | Dunmar writes letters to his neighbours asking them to refuse. Two answer; one passes his letter to the Lord-Marshal's office. |
| 1463 | The Ash Company, under Hask's lieutenant **Kitford Whitlock**, marches to the Cinder Hills and burns Dunmarrow in a single night. The wells are fouled, the keep is fired and the gatehouse split with a ram. Dunmar dies at the gate. Only the lower keep survives. |
| 1463-1464 | Survivors scatter to Hadrun, Cinder Ford and the lowland towns. The Marshal's office lists the town as abandoned. The other hill lords pay their tithe thereafter. |
| 1491 | Twenty-eight years on, the Order of the Banked Fire takes the ruins as its hall. |

## How it is told
- **Chancery version:** a rebel lord refused a lawful levy and the fire spread out of control.
- **The hill-folk:** the Ash Company chose a night when the gates stood open for a feast. They tell of children counted on the road afterwards.
- **Ulric Vane,** a mason's boy then, tends the graves and says the names aloud: twenty-three headstones among far more dead.

## DM secrets
- Dunmar's real reason for refusing the tithe was not defiance. His steward had learned from a Pyrehouse deserter that the Spire's condemned were being fed to something, and that the tithe was part of it. The letters in the vault strongbox (see dunmarrow) say so plainly, naming the Pyrehouse and the "Last Ember".
- The Choir saw to it that Dunmar's letter reached the Lord-Marshal's office and that Hask sent Whitlock with a full company. The Ember Rolls record the burning as the largest single feeding of the coal in the Ash Reign.
- **Kitford Whitlock** died in 1477 of what looked like a wasting fever; it was the burn's slow early signs. His sword hangs in the Marshal's hall.
- The sealed vault was Dunmar's last act: he hid the letters in a Velun-era strongroom from Hask. It is now where the Coal rests (see heartcoal), and the irony is intended.
- Hask never spoke of Dunmarrow. Lorard, thirteen at the time, rode with the camp. DM option: he remembers the road afterwards.
