The Vent Games are Steamside's yearly games and betting day at the Third Vent, held on the first quiet afternoon after the harvest tithe. They are a commons festival: no crown, no Pyrehouse, no Watch purse. Nobody dies, and quarrels are settled on the ash instead of in the cells. For one afternoon a year, Steamside's bath-hands and dye-workers, the Ashward's pit-hands, the Quay's porters, hill-folk, old soldiers and the odd off-duty Redcollar lean on the same rope. Each brought its own wrestling, and Kharun counts seven roots of it.

## The place
- **The Third Vent:** a stone-ringed hollow at the end of Steamside, where the street dips and hot mist rolls up from a crack in the basalt. A line of bright rags hangs over the steam, and stalls sell hot cider and eggs boiled in the vent's mist.
- **The ring:** a ten-pace circle of packed ash and sand beside the crack, with a chalk line and a rope fence the crowd leans on. The ash is swept from Steamside's streets, which makes it the king's snow, the city's dead come down from the Long Smoke. Wrestlers throw a pinch of salt on it before they grip.
- **The Hollis bathhouse:** the old dead-washers' house on the vent's lip, in the Hollis family since about 1300.
  - **The Spoon Wall** in the front room has one wooden spoon for every champion since the 1330s, each with the name burned into the bowl. The oldest are black and cracked, and the spoon for 1467 hangs blank.
  - **The Kettle Pool** is the hottest pool, fed straight from the vent. The Long Soak ends the day there.
- **The slate:** Rosa's rules and brackets, chalked on a slab of roofing slate propped on the bathhouse step, with the book of wagers beside it. The old games are chalked on the back.

## The seven roots
Kharun wrestles seven ways, one for each people that leans on the rope. The Short Open mixes them in one quick bout. The Long Open gives each its own ground: the Rope, then the Collar, then the Ash. The full rules are in rules-of-the-grudge-pit.
- **The Push** (Steamside's bath-hands; the oldest root, from the hot-water quarrels, with the Hollis salt-haulers' Strap start): go out of the circle or touch the ash with anything but your feet and you lose the fall. Stamp, salt, crouch and charge. *(Sumo, with ssireum's set grip.)*
- **The Belt** (the Cinder Hills, since 1424): harness grips with thigh loops, hooks and heel trips; stay upright and keep stepping. *(Glima.)*
- **The Hawk** (the South March and the Shaar, by way of the Ash Companies, c. 1458): an open start, falls on an elbow, knee or back, leg grabs, no weight classes, and the hawk dance. *(Bökh.)*
- **The Gorget** (the Watch, from the Collarhouse yard): grips above the belt only, as if you wore gorget and greaves; the big lifts, the turns and the body locks. *(Greco-Roman.)*
- **The Linen** (the dye-works lanes, c. 1438): smocks and sashes, collar-and-sleeve grips and foot sweeps. A clean throw flat on the back, a full fall, ends it. *(Judo.)*
- **The Catch** (the Ashward's pit-hands, c. 1395): legs, trips, rides and turns, and the pin. Every takedown scores marks. *(Freestyle and folkstyle; catch-as-catch-can.)*
- **The Knot** (Brimstone Quay, off the Calishite galleys and caravans): fight off your back, sweep, pass, then lock or strangle until they slap the ash. Barred from the Short Open since the Strangle; it has its own ground in the Long Open. *(Brazilian jiu-jitsu.)*

## Customs
- **The Ash Rule:** what's settled on the ash stays settled. Two who wrestle a grudge shake on the chalk and soak side by side at the Long Soak. Raising the quarrel again afterwards is raising it against Rosa, and Steamside takes her side.
- **Salt and stamp:** before the Grip each wrestler stamps twice and throws a pinch of Saltreach salt on the ash, "to keep the dead out of the fight".
- **The spoon and the fingers:** Rosa judges with Old Marl's wooden spoon. She counts a pin on her fingers, and the spoon comes down on her tub when a bout is decided.
- **The fifth:** a fifth of every wager has always gone somewhere: to the Salt Reeve's man (1371–1453), to the scald tin (1453–1491), and since day 3 of 1491 to the burn-sick tin under Rosa's counter. A disqualified wrestler's stake goes in the tin too.
- **No Pyrehouse:** "The Pyrehouse burns the dead. At my vent we wash the living." No warden has stood on the ash in living memory.
- **What the champion gets, and owes:**
  - a spoon on the wall;
  - the next round of cider for the rail, at the champion's expense;
  - one challenger named for next year's opening bout (the **Champion's Grudge**, where the challenger calls the Way);
  - first into the Kettle Pool at the Soak;
  - the Factor's Cup.
- **The old games:** the Hollis Stone, the Bucket Line and the Bellow are still chalked on the back of the slate. When the rail shouts for one loudly enough, Rosa runs it before the Soak, and a champion's asking usually does it.
- **The bell:** since 1454 the Soak has ended at the curfew bell, and everyone still in the water shares the towel.
- **Sashes, rags and taps** (the Long Open): the first-named wrestler wears a rust sash and the second a blue one. Two judges at the rope's corners hold rust and blue rags, by custom one from Steamside and one from the Ashward. Rosa cautions a wrestler with a tap of the spoon on the shoulder.
- **The long and short of it:** a 12-mark lead ends a Long bout at the next stoppage, and Rosa says her line as she calls it.
- **Gestures:** after the closing salute the winner may do their root's gesture: the hawk dance, a Push squat and clap, a dyer's bow. Taunting earns a spoon-tap.

## Day 12, 1491
- **The slate:** "Three games, three prizes": the egg race, the plank-walk and the grudge pit, then the Long Soak at dusk. The children's egg heat ran in the morning, won by **Wren Tolly**, a ten-year-old Ashward girl. The rest of the egg race and the plank-walk run after the pit.
- **The Open** (odds at the first Grip: Hesta 2 to 1, Chadickith 6 to 1, Dorrik 6 to 1, the rest 8 to 1 or longer):
  - **Quarterfinals:**
    - Chadickith beat **Dorrik Pell** by Out (a circle-and-grip, then shoved over the chalk).
    - Big Hesta beat **Corra Nettle**, a bathhouse laundress, by Down.
    - **Tess Aldin** beat **Bram Colk** by Down (a sidestep, a heel-hook trip and a pin).
    - **Joss Marl** beat **Ilk Venn** by Down (he took Ilk's back and rode him down).
  - **Semifinals:**
    - Chadickith beat Big Hesta by Down (a body-lock suplex, a pounce and three fingers). She let her fistful of ash fall rather than foul.
    - Tess beat Joss by Out (his wet grip slipped and he stumbled over the chalk).
  - **Final:** Chadickith beat Tess by Down (a single-leg counter, a long fight from the Kneel, a corkscrew turn and three fingers). She cried "Ash" as the third finger came down.
- **Paid:** Chadickith took the champion's purse of 20 crowns. His 5-crown bet on himself at 6 to 1 paid 30 more plus his stake back, 55 crowns in all. Tess took 6 crowns, and Hesta and Joss 2 each.
- **Customs paid:** the spoon is burned and hung; Chadickith bought the rail's cider (3 crowns) and named **Marda Ironside** as his 1492 challenger. Rosa announced the Long Open the same moment. Still to come: the Factor's Cup.
- **Rosa's rulings on the day:** a heel on the chalk is over the chalk; not down, not counted; a trunk is a hand; a chop is a strike. The full list is in rules-of-the-grudge-pit.

## The people
- **Rosa Hollis** (rosa-hollis): Keeper of the Slate since 1480; host, judge and keeper of the book.
- **Old Wenna Hollis** (old-wenna-hollis): Rosa's mother, Keeper 1452–1480. Nearly blind, she sits wrapped in towels on a stool by the vent and remembers every bout.
- **Nella Hollis:** Rosa's daughter (born 1466), who runs the pools and will take the slate one day.
- **Pip Hollis:** Nella's son, twelve, the chalk-boy, who keeps the brackets and the marks and runs bets.
- **Annet Sallow:** a scrivener's widow who keeps a second copy of the book of wagers, so nobody can say the Hollis book is crooked.
- **Mother Seld:** Steamside's bonesetter and midwife, on the step with splints and brine whenever the pit runs.
- **Nan Grell:** a cider-seller with a broad back and a bad eye, who minds wrestlers' gear behind her kegs.
- **The 1491 wrestlers:** Big Hesta (big-hesta), Tess Aldin (tess-aldin), Dorrik Pell (dorrik-pell), Joss Marl (joss-marl), Bram Colk (bram-colk), Ilk Venn (ilk-venn), and Corra Nettle.
- **Old champions still about:** Wynot Northcott (wynot-northcott), the Anvil; Marda Ironside (marda-ironside), the only Redcollar champion; Teo Varrow (teo-varrow), the Quay's Knot-man; and Gorm Halfhand (gorm-halfhand), the Hawk's grand old man.
- **Gil Brackwater:** a Kettle Cone charcoal-carter who wrestles the Belt and gave Rosa its rules.
- **House Mostana** (house-mostana) sends the Factor's Cup.

## Why it matters now
- **The elder vote is tomorrow.** The Lamplit Council's first sitting is at noon on day 13, and before the real vote each region names an elder pro tem by acclamation. Today's rail at the Third Vent is the Kharun commons (Steamside, the Quay and the middle-terrace households), with the Ashward in the same crowd.
- **The likely elders are both here.** Rosa is the commons' likeliest voice, and the Ashward's likeliest, Wynot Northcott, is a former champion. Whoever the rail cheers carries weight.
- **Chadickith has the rail.** He proposed the elder seats at the noon council of day 10, and today he became the rail's champion, the giant's boy who won it fair under Rosa's spoon.

## DM secrets
- **The Sergeant's Fall was the burn.** Brannoc Hale's eyes "caught red like a cat's at the lamp" (Wenna's words), a sign nobody could name until the roc. Since day 3 Wenna has understood what she saw, and that the Fury Rule was written against the burn decades before anyone named it.
- **The Strangle and the house:** a week after the Strangle, House Mostana's factor of the day paid 50 crowns into the scald tin "in friendship", and the guard's name was never chalked. The Factor's Cup (1479) is the same habit in silver: the house keeps a friendly eye on the one day the commons gather.
- **The Dead-Washers' Ledger:** Old Marl's first rules are written on the blank backs of the Third Vent dead-washers' ledger from the Grey Cough, kept in a cedar box behind the Spoon Wall. Its entries note "dead that would not lie still, with the heat still in them". That is close to the early signs of the burn, like Kasette Mercer's journal in the Pyrehouse library. Wenna knows it is there. Left open, as with Mercer: foreshadowing, or only plague.
- **Rosa's debt to House Mostana, and her cold room:** see rosa-hollis.
- **This year's Cup:** Seipora Mostana means to carry it to the forge herself (see house-mostana).
- **The Long Open will start a quarrel.** Old Wenna banned strangles in 1467 and hates seeing them come back, even in the Ash with Rosa's spoon on the shoulder; she'll argue it at the Soak. Teo Varrow has wanted it for twenty years.
- **The Hawk and the soldiers:** some of Lorard's three hundred men inside the walls wrestle the Hawk, and a few have already asked Gorm Halfhand whether soldiers may enter in 1492. A company's men in a commons festival is exactly what the Sergeant's Fall taught Steamside to fear.
