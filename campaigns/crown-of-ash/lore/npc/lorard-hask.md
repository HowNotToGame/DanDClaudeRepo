# Lorard Hask, the Lord-Marshal
Forty-one, broad, grey at the temples, with a soldier's limp from a Shaar spear. He wears plain field plate with no crest and eats standing up. He was born in a mercenary camp three years before his father took the throne and was acknowledged as the king's firstborn at seven. For twenty years he has been half-exiled to the South March for openly opposing the Sky Gallows.

## Claim and power base
- **Claim:** firstborn son, publicly acknowledged by the king. Border Kingdoms custom has always respected the sword-heir.
- **Weakness:** he is "camp-born." No temple ever blessed his parents' handfast, and the old nobility call him a sellsword's bastard.
- **Power:** the **Ash Companies**, about 1,200 professional soldiers in the field army (many of them sons of the original sellswords) plus the March garrisons. Most of his soldiers would die for him. He pays them on time, eats what they eat and remembers their names.

## Public face
Blunt, honest, tired. He promises to tear down the Sky Gallows, end the Pyre-tithe and "make Ossmaar a fortress, not a slaughterhouse." He openly scorns the Ember Law as "my father's conjuring trick."

## Want, fear, voice
- **Wants:** the throne, without having to sack his own capital. Under that, the approval of a father who never gave it.
- **Fears:** becoming his father, a man who could order a hundred deaths in that soft voice and then sleep.
- **Voice and quirk:** short, clipped sentences. Counts things out loud: soldiers, coins, the dead. Calls everyone by trade or rank ("Smith's boy." "Sergeant." "Priest."). Cracks his knuckles one at a time before he says something hard.

## Sympathetic / monstrous
- He genuinely protects his soldiers and the frontier villages, and he stood up to the king when no one else would.
- **The Thornwater Tithe (1484 DR):** after a revolt, he hanged every tenth man in Thornwater, sixty-one men in all, to stop his father sending the whole town to the Spire. He still believes it saved lives, and Thornwater still spits at his name.

## What has happened since the king died
- **The ultimatum:** the day after the king's death he gave Kharun ten days to open its gates or be stormed, and everyone who remembered Thornwater believed him.
- **The Great Pyre (night of day 2):** he stood in the square when the Gallowsbird struck. He saw Chadickith throw the bound Brother Garn out to the roc as bait, which hardened his opinion of the loxodon, and then saw him organise the evacuation, which softened it a little. He sent the rider that brought four light bolt-throwers through the Saltgate wicket, and lost his sergeant and the engine crews when the roc came down on them.
- **The gates (day 9):** Halda's captains voted four to one to open the gates on her terms. At noon a column of three hundred marched in orderly by the Saltgate; the rest of the army still camps in the Lowfields. Lorard came to the Collarhouse himself.
- **The forge search (dawn, day 10):** told that the Watch and "the giant's smith" had stolen the Coal, he came to search Okton's forge with twelve of the Ash Companies. Halda arrived with proof that his quartermaster **Unara** had sent a runner, Ashlo, to "make a spark" at the forge so the search would have cause. Unara confessed that a grey-shawled woman of the Choir had approached her first with silver. Lorard stripped her rank on the spot, cut her quartermaster's cord, and ordered her held in irons for trial by a vote of her own company. He asked Okton straight out whether the Coal was there. Okton answered: "It was. It is not now. I will not say where." Lorard accepted it and stood his men down.
- **The noon council (day 10):** he disowned Unara's scheme. When Isine gave the king's last words, he called her "the king's hand", not a thief, and asked Coldwell where he had been when the king spoke. He voted for the **Lamplit Council** as regency. Under it he commands all arms and holds a Marshal's lamp; the army's pay is guaranteed from the Keep treasury; three hundred of his men stay inside the walls. He backed hearth-voted elder seats guarded by his sergeants and the Watch together.
- **Now (evening, day 11):** inside Kharun, waiting on the first sitting (day 13). His opinion of Chadickith is mixed: harder for Garn, softer for the evacuation and the forge.

## DM secrets
- **He is not Ivoard Hask's son.** His real father was Pellot Rook, Hask's sword-brother, who died in the South March campaign of 1450 DR before Lorard was born. Hask married the pregnant Mara and raised the boy as his own. Lorard learned the truth two years ago from his dying mother's letter. Since then he has had the last living witness, the old company surgeon who delivered him, pushed from a wall at Fort Vell. **He kept the letter**, because he couldn't burn his mother's last words. It is in a locked dispatch case in his command tent. If it surfaces, his claim dies, and his soldiers' faith may die with it.
- **Rook's confession (day 11):** Captain Hesard Rook asked to see him alone in his cell and greeted him as "cousin": Pellot was Hesard's uncle. Hesard admitted feeding the Choir news of the forge because Coldwell swore the Pyrehouse would back Lorard for the crown if the Coal were recovered. Lorard said nothing for a long time, then: "I know what I am. I never asked you to bleed for it." He did not forgive him, and did not call for the rope. **Chadickith heard every word** from behind the grate, and Captain Marda Ironside wrote it down. Chadickith is keeping it as leverage. Marda holds the record sealed and unread in her strongbox and will give it to Halda on day 14 unless Chadickith changes that. (Whether Lorard realised who stood behind the grate isn't recorded; decide when it matters.)
- **He truly did not know** about Unara's scheme.
- He doesn't know what the Coal is, only distrusts it on instinct. He planned to wear it once for the crowd and then drop it in the Lake of Steam.
- **He is not infected.** (The v1 secret that battlefield pyres left him faintly Touched is retired: ash and smoke carry no burn under the v2 rules. Destroying the Coal only stops new infections; it would not kill Kitwin or Okton.)
- House Mostana is quietly lending him gold for his soldiers' pay, so the house backs him and Oselle at once. He knows, and he thinks he's using them. The regency's guarantee of army pay from the treasury makes that loan less necessary, which Mostana will notice.
- Sergeant Wilash Redhand of the Watch passes Watch news to his quartermaster's office, now without Unara.
- He respects Okton, whose plate he has worn for years.
