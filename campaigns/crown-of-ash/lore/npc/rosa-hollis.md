Rosa Hollis (born 1443) keeps the Hollis bathhouse on the lip of the Third Vent in Steamside, and the slate of the Vent Games, as Hollis keepers have since Old Marl chalked the first circle about 1330. Half of Steamside brings its quarrels to her steps.

## Look and voice
- Broad and loud, with forearms scalded pink from the vents, a scalded-pink apron and a bellow of a laugh. She judges from an upturned tub with Old Marl's wooden spoon.
- She counts points on her fingers, ends a ruling with "and that's the long and short of it", and calls people "love". Her stock rulings: "Not down, not counted!" and "A heel on the chalk is over the chalk."
- She won the Bellow seven times as a girl (1456–1462).

## What she wants and fears
- **Wants:** Steamside's quarrels settled on her steps, not in the Watch's cells or the Pyrehouse's hands; the Games kept honest and loud; the bathhouse passed to Nella whole.
- **Fears:** the burn reaching her patrons, and a fire in the bathhouse. The Hollis roof burned in 1402, and she was raised on the story.
- **Lines she won't cross:**
  - no warden on the ash ("The Pyrehouse burns the dead; at my vent we wash the living");
  - no crooked book;
  - nobody anointed at her vent.

## Family and household
- **Old Wenna Hollis** (old-wenna-hollis): her mother, Keeper of the Slate 1452–1480.
- **Nella Hollis:** her daughter (born 1466), who runs the pools.
- **Pip Hollis:** Nella's son, twelve, the chalk-boy.
- **Edric:** her husband, who died in 1479 in a pocket of vent gas on the shore.
- **The household:** Annet Sallow keeps the second book, Mother Seld sets bones on the step, and Corra Nettle does the bathhouse laundry. Corra drew Big Hesta in the day-12 quarterfinal.

## As Keeper (since 1480)
- She took the slate when Wenna's eyes went.
- In 1481 she dropped the Hollis Stone after a dyer's boy broke his foot.
- In 1486 she ruled that the Open counts a wrestler held on the ash whether or not their legs are wrapped round the holder (the Varrow ruling).
- From 1489 she drafted **the Seven Roots slate** (the Long Open: the Rope, the Collar, the Ash, and the Last Fall), asking each quarter for its rules. She meant to unveil it in 1490, but the Quiet Year silenced it.
- She ran the Silent Games of 1490, and after the roc she renamed the scald tin the burn-sick tin.
- On day 12, when Chadickith named Marda Ironside as his challenger, she announced the Long Open from her tub: the Open's final and the Champion's Grudge are wrestled long from 1492.
- On day 12 of 1491 she ran the first loud Games in two years:
  - she polled the rail and priced the giant's boy at 6 to 1;
  - she ruled a trunk a hand and a chop a strike;
  - she paid Chadickith 55 crowns.

## The elder seat
- She is Okton and Isine's guess for the Kharun commons elder. She hasn't said she wants it.
- The Ash Rule (what's settled on the ash stays settled) is the closest thing Steamside has to a law of its own, and it's hers.

## DM secrets
- **She owes House Mostana 300 crowns.**
  - The Quiet Year emptied the pools: the cure-trade from across the lake dried up when word went round that the king was dying.
  - In Ches 1491 she borrowed the money to re-pipe the Kettle Pool and re-roof the front room. It falls due at the Feast of the Moon.
  - Factor Seipora calls it "a friendship" and hasn't pressed.
  - Nobody knows, not even Nella. An elder in debt to a foreign house would be a scandal, or a leash.
- **The cold room:** since day 3 she has let two burn-sick neighbours sit in the cold-spring room under the bathhouse at night.
  - They are Ansel Tarrant, a dye-worker, and Liss Vary, a laundress, both at Stirring.
  - Cold water and quiet help them keep their tempers, nothing more.
  - She's afraid the Choir will find them, and she'd take the Order's help if it were offered without fuss.
- **The ledger:** she knows the Dead-Washers' Ledger is in the cedar box behind the Spoon Wall, and has never read past the first page.
