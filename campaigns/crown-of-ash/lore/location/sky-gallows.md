# The Sky Gallows
A black basalt spire two hundred feet tall, three miles south of Kharun at the end of the overgrown Gallows Road. A switchback stair cut into the rock climbs to a flat top ringed with iron posts and heavy chains. From about 1401 to 1490 DR, first under the last Velun kings and then under Ivoard Hask, the realm's condemned were chained up there, dusted with pyre-ash by a Pyrewarden, and left "for the sky to judge." The sky usually sent the Gallowsbird.

- The stair is cracked and the chains are rusted. The roc tore several away. Bones lie in the scree below.
- Locals won't go near it. Shepherds used to say the roc still landed there to wait; the Gallowsbird is dead now (night of day 2).
- **Day 5:** the Order's runners sheltered in a rock overhang in the scree at its foot with the Coal through the day. A three-man Marshal's patrol rode up to water their horses, found fresh tracks, and was bluffed: the runners passed as armed couriers with a crate of "fresh forge iron", and the riders reported only "three armed couriers with a crate going south."
- **Things to do:** find the carved last words of a Burners' Rising rebel (1423) or a Velun Plot noble (1474); discover that the Ash Anointing was a ritual, not mere custom; bait the bird; or hide something where no one will look.

## DM secrets
- King Uzhar Velun raised the Spire's chains around 1401 on the Glad Choir's advice, to feed the coal hidden in the Pyrehouse crypt. Most people credit the Gallows to Hask, who made it infamous. The very old know better.
- Near the top is an inscription scratched in 1472 by a condemned Pyrewarden acolyte: *"The ash is not for the dead. The ash is for the bird."* It is the first clue that the anointing was deliberate.
- (Retired: the roc-trap idea. The Gallowsbird is dead.)
- A patrol sergeant tapped the casket's lid at the Spire's foot and found it warm. If the Marshal's camp ever compares notes, "three couriers with a warm crate going south" is a thread.
